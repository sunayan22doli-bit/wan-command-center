Walk me through designing a WAN for a new 5-site global deployment where each site needs 100+ Gbps with full redundancy.Architecture

What they're testing: Can you think through architecture holistically? Do you consider redundancy from the start? Can you explain trade-offs?

Key points to hit

  • Identify topology (mesh vs. hub-spoke)
  • Specify carriers and circuit diversity
  • Address failure scenarios
  • Discuss cost vs. resilience
  • Mention failover mechanisms

Simplified answer

  • Topology: partial mesh — hub DC (2x400G) + spokes (2x100G each) ≈ 2.4 Tbps aggregate
  • Carriers: 3 minimum, split 40/40/20% — no single-carrier dependency
  • Fiber: verify physically diverse routes, no shared conduits
  • Redundancy: active-active preferred, <5 sec failover convergence
  • Cost: roughly $15M/year for the 5-site build
  • Roll out in phases: 50% -> 75% -> 100% capacity over 6 months
Your company leases dark fiber between two DCs. Explain DWDM and when you'd use it vs. buying ISP circuits.Optical / ROI

What they're testing: Do you understand optical systems? Can you calculate ROI on infrastructure? Do you know when to build vs. buy?

Simplified answer

  • ISP circuits: fast, no capex, but pricier long-term (~$2.4M/yr for 1 Tbps)
  • Dark fiber + DWDM: ~$480K in year 1, ~$330K/yr after — breaks even in about 3 months
  • Use dark fiber when: 3+ year commitment, 500+ Gbps need, you own both endpoints
  • Use ISP circuits when: short-term, variable demand, or diversity isn't possible
  • For IREN: dark fiber for the core DC-to-DC paths, ISP circuits for growth and flexibility
A customer has intermittent connectivity issues — occasional packet loss on a circuit. Walk me through troubleshooting and escalation.Ops / SLA

What they're testing: Can you diagnose network issues? How do you escalate effectively? Do you understand SLA management?

Simplified answer

  • Confirm scope first: how much loss, how long, which circuit — isolate to one ISP with direct ping tests
  • Congestion (high utilization, no errors) vs. equipment failure (errors present)
  • Escalate in order: service desk -> account manager (if no callback in 2h) -> demand a fix and a capacity upgrade
  • Communicate early: tell the customer what's wrong, what you did (rerouted traffic), and what's next
  • Prevent recurrence: rebalance traffic shares, add a third carrier, cap any one ISP at 40% of traffic
IREN is growing GPU capacity from 50,000 to 200,000 over 18 months. How do you ensure WAN capacity keeps pace?Capacity planning

What they're testing: Can you think ahead strategically? Do you understand infrastructure dependencies? Can you build roadmaps?

Simplified answer

  • Model demand: translate GPU-count growth into a bandwidth curve, today vs. target
  • Circuits take 6-12 weeks (ISP) or months (dark fiber) — order now for month 6-8 delivery
  • Phase the rollout in 6-month steps rather than jumping straight to 4x capacity
  • A hybrid of ISP + dark fiber saves roughly half the cost of an ISP-only build at this scale
  • Flag the risks either way: growth slower (stage the orders) / faster (pre-qualify backup ISPs) / new region (relationships already in place)

Rapid-fire concept checks

04

60-second answers — know these cold.

BGP — what and why?

Border Gateway Protocol. Used by ISPs to route internet traffic; affects failover speed and traffic engineering. BGP failures can prevent failover.

Active-active vs. active-passive

Active-active: both circuits in use simultaneously (preferred, full capacity always used). Active-passive: standby circuit used only if primary fails.

Typical ISP circuit lead time?

6–12 weeks depending on size: 100Gbps 6–8 weeks, 400Gbps 10–12 weeks. Why you order far in advance.

What's an IX and why does IREN care?

Internet Exchange — a peering point where multiple carriers meet. Lower cost than dedicated circuits to each peer; cheap connectivity to cloud providers.

What is "route diversity"?

Connectivity via 2+ geographically independent paths. If one path fails, traffic reroutes automatically — protects against single points of failure.

What is dark fiber?

Unlit fiber leased from a provider; you bring your own optical equipment. Lower cost long-term, more complex. IREN use case: core DC-to-DC paths.

How do you measure carrier SLA compliance?

Monitor uptime %, latency, packet loss; compare to contract terms (usually 99.9% uptime); track misses: "ISP X missed SLA twice this year."

What is "carrier consolidation"?

Moving more business to fewer carriers to gain negotiating leverage. "If you perform, you get 80% of our volume."

Why would IREN need "fiber diversity"?

Same route through the ground = single point of failure, even across different ISPs. Want 2+ independent physical paths to protect against accidental cuts.

What is BGP "prepending"?

Making one path appear artificially longer than another so traffic prefers the shorter path — used for load balancing between carriers.

Define "convergence time"

Time from failure detection to traffic rerouting during failover. Measured in seconds — ideally <5 sec. Shorter = less customer impact.

What is a colocation facility?

A neutral facility where multiple carriers colocate equipment; your DC gear can connect to multiple carriers there. Lower-cost peering for IREN.

$100M WAN budget for IREN — allocate it?

60% ISP circuits ($60M), 30% dark fiber infrastructure ($30M), 10% redundancy/contingency ($10M). Balances flexibility with cost optimization.

Explain BGP communities

Tags on routes that let routers prioritize. Multi-carrier example: Carrier A local-pref 200, Carrier B local-pref 100 — traffic prefers A until failure, then shifts to B automatically.

Why does dark fiber take so long?

Permitting (2–4mo) + construction (2–4mo) + splicing/testing (1–2mo). Can't compress permitting — that's government. Get creative: pre-negotiate permits, position equipment early, parallel construction.

What is network slicing?

5G concept: isolate traffic on shared infrastructure with guaranteed SLAs per slice. Not mainstream in enterprise yet, but could support customer SLA differentiation at IREN in 3–5 years.

If they ask something you don't know?

“Great question — I haven't worked with that directly, but I'd approach it by [logical method]. In my experience, [related lesson]. I'd research it if this becomes part of the role.” Then move on, don't dwell.

3 days before a critical deployment, a circuit you counted on is delayed 2 weeks. The customer is threatening to leave. How do you handle it?Crisis

Evaluation rubric: 1★ panic/blame/no solutions · 3★ options identified but unclear decision path · 5★ clear crisis plan, multiple options with tradeoffs, stakeholder alignment.

Simplified answer

  • First 2 hours: confirm the delay is real, find the root cause, check alternate carriers, tell your boss immediately
  • Build 3-4 real options with tradeoffs: delay / pay for backup / partial launch / push the carrier harder
  • Escalate to the carrier VP with a clear dollar-impact statement
  • Call the customer directly, be honest about the options, let them choose the tradeoff
  • Execute the choice, give daily updates both ways, run a post-incident review
Infrastructure Deployment needs a new DC live in 6 weeks, but carriers say 12 weeks minimum. How do you navigate it?Cross-functional

Simplified answer

  • Acknowledge the real business need behind the deadline before pushing back on it
  • Get carriers' actual best-case timeline, not their standard quote
  • Present 3 real options with probabilities and tradeoffs: crash program / partial launch / delay
  • Make it a business decision for leadership to choose — not a technical "no"
  • Execute whichever option is chosen, without relitigating it

Dark fiber vs. carrier circuit provisioning — how are they different operationally?Dark fiber
  • Dark fiber: you own the equipment, 18-24 month lead time, $2M+ upfront, scales via DWDM without new fiber
  • Lit circuits: carrier-managed, 6-12 week lead time, lower upfront cost, capped speed, vendor lock-in
  • Use dark fiber for long-haul backbone with predictable, high growth; lit circuits for fast or uncertain-demand needs
  • Healthy portfolio mix: roughly 30% dark fiber, 50% lit circuits, 20% backup/alternative paths
Have you worked in a hyperscale environment? What's different about connectivity there?Hyperscale
  • Bandwidth is orders of magnitude larger — Tbps scale, 10-50 carriers per major site just to get enough capacity
  • Growth is exponential (100%+ YoY) — plan for 3x, not the 20% YoY of traditional enterprise
  • Decisions move fast — carrier agreements are pre-negotiated, not renegotiated deal by deal
  • Redundancy is N+2/N+3 with true geographic diversity, not just N+1
  • At that volume you have real leverage — carriers compete hard to keep hyperscale business
Explain DWDM and long-haul fiber transport. When would you use it?DWDM
  • Multiplexes many wavelengths onto one fiber — scale capacity by adding transponders, not new fiber
  • Makes dark fiber's economics work: cheap to scale from 100G to 1 Tbps over a few years vs. buying more fiber
  • Essential on long-haul routes, since dedicating separate fiber per customer isn't economical
  • For IREN: this is how you scale DC-to-DC backbone capacity without repeated multi-year fiber builds
Route diversity — how do you verify it physically?Diversity
  • Don't trust a carrier's word that two circuits are "diverse" — verify with route maps and on-site GPS checks
  • Real catch: two "diverse" fibers shared the same 40-mile conduit — one cut would've taken both down
  • Fixed it by forcing a truly independent path from a different carrier before go-live
  • Always run an actual failover test before production, and re-verify diversity periodically
Colocation cross-connects vs. circuits — what's the difference?Colocation
  • Cross-connect: short physical link within one colo connecting your gear to a carrier's — cheap ($500-2K), fast (days)
  • Circuit: the carrier's long-haul connection out of that colo — expensive, slow to provision
  • Use VLAN tagging to run multiple carriers over one physical port at a colo
  • Keep at least 2-3 carrier cross-connects per site for redundancy, and validate each before go-live
How do you forecast bandwidth needs 12-24 months out for AI/GPU infrastructure?Forecasting

  • Model best/base/worst-case scenarios and order for the worst case with 1.5-2x headroom
  • Match circuit type to lead time: lit circuits ~2 months notice, dark fiber ~5 months notice
  • Talk to customers and sales directly — they know pipeline and deal sizes better than a spreadsheet
  • Re-forecast every quarter; you'll always be somewhat wrong, so overprovision
Backbone infrastructure vs. regional fiber — how do they differ?Architecture
  • Backbone: long-haul, high-capacity, strategic routes between major regions — worth owning (dark fiber + DWDM)
  • Regional: shorter, tactical, access-focused links — better bought as lit circuits for flexibility
  • Backbone concentrates risk (a failure there hits everything downstream), so it needs the most redundancy investment
  • Rule of thumb: own what you'll fully utilize for 5+ years; rent everything else
What SLA metrics do you always include in carrier negotiations?SLA

  • Define every term precisely — how "down" is measured, whose monitoring counts, what's excluded
  • Attach real financial credits to misses (5-25% of monthly fee, scaling with severity)
  • Include escalation tiers with response-time commitments and a termination-for-repeated-breach clause

Technical credibility

Layer 1 vs. Layer 3 diversity — why do you need both?Diversity
  • Layer 1 (physical fiber path) protects against fiber cuts; Layer 3 (BGP/routing) protects against carrier network failures
  • Having only one leaves a gap — same-carrier diverse fiber can still both go down if that carrier's network fails
  • True resilience needs different physical routes AND different carriers/ASNs announcing them
  • Verify both: route maps + GPS-confirmed divergence for Layer 1, BGP path checks and failover tests for Layer 3
When would you recommend IX connectivity vs. direct carrier circuits?IX
  • Cheap, fast-to-deploy cross-connects to many carriers at once in a shared facility
  • Great for backup paths, non-critical/batch traffic, and low-cost geographic load-balancing
  • Weak for primary customer-facing paths — mostly best-effort, no strong SLA, shared infrastructure
  • Best used alongside an owned backbone: dark fiber for critical paths, IX for flexible/backup capacity
Explain BGP communities and how you'd use them for multi-carrier traffic engineering.BGP
  • Tag routes per carrier/path so routers automatically know which to prefer
  • Set local-preference so one carrier is primary and another is automatic backup — no manual failover needed
  • Can also steer specific traffic types (e.g. inference vs. batch) down different paths for cost or latency reasons
  • Document the scheme clearly — undocumented BGP policy becomes unmanageable fast
How would you design a global anycast network for AI inference? WAN implications?Anycast
  • Anycast serves each request from the nearest healthy site using one shared IP address
  • Cuts inference latency automatically by routing to the closest GPU cluster
  • Doesn't fix a weak backbone — every anycast site still needs strong, low-latency connectivity
  • Requires tight BGP coordination across sites so failover between them is fast and clean
MPLS vs. pure IP — when would you use each, and what are the tradeoffs?MPLS
  • MPLS: guaranteed bandwidth and prioritized traffic, carrier-managed, but pricier
  • Pure IP/internet transit: cheaper and flexible, but best-effort with no guaranteed path
  • Use MPLS for latency-sensitive production paths that need an SLA-backed guarantee
  • Use pure IP for cost-sensitive, tolerant traffic like batch data movement or backup paths
Explain VLAN tagging and Q-in-Q in a colocation cross-connect scenario.VLAN
  • VLAN tagging lets multiple carriers share one physical port, each isolated on its own tag
  • Q-in-Q nests a customer's VLANs inside your own — useful if you resell connectivity inside a colo
  • Practical benefit: avoids paying for a separate physical cross-connect per carrier when ports are limited
How would you architect multi-path load balancing across carriers? Operational challenges?Load balancing
  • BGP multipath or policy routing can split traffic across two or more carriers at once
  • Watch for asymmetric routing breaking stateful services when inbound/outbound paths differ
  • Need real-time path health visibility to shift load before a degrading path hurts the customer
  • Even "backup" capacity used for load-balancing needs an SLA good enough not to degrade the experience
What's network slicing, and how might it apply to IREN?Network slicing
  • Isolates traffic into virtual networks with guaranteed SLAs on shared physical infrastructure
  • Mostly a 5G/telco concept today, not mainstream in enterprise WAN
  • Possible future fit: differentiated SLA tiers for premium inference customers without separate physical networks
  • Worth tracking over the next 3-5 years, not worth building for today
Pure dark fiber vs. lit fiber — when would IREN use each?Dark vs. lit
  • Dark fiber: owned, long-term, high-growth backbone between IREN's own data centers
  • Lit fiber: regional access and customer-facing links where speed of deployment matters more than unit cost
  • Rule of thumb: own the paths you'll fully use for 5+ years; rent everything else
How would you monitor WAN quality end-to-end? What metrics matter most?Monitoring
  • Availability and packet loss per circuit and per path, tracked continuously against SLA, not just at failure
  • Latency and jitter measured end-to-end (what the application experiences), not just to the carrier handoff
  • Utilization trending to catch capacity issues before they become outages
  • Run your own independent monitoring — don't rely solely on carrier-reported numbers
When would you use wavelength services (private wavelengths) vs. traditional circuits?Wavelengths
  • A private wavelength is a dedicated slice of a carrier's DWDM system — more capacity/control than a shared lit circuit
  • Fits the middle ground: more than a standard lit circuit gives you, but not enough volume/timeline to justify owning dark fiber
  • Use it for 100G+ routes where dark fiber capex isn't yet justified
Would you evaluate SD-WAN for a hyperscale deployment? Pros and cons?SD-WAN
  • Pros: centralized policy control, automatic path selection across carriers, fast to deploy at new sites
  • Cons: adds a software/appliance layer and vendor dependency; not built for massive point-to-point DC-to-DC backbone capacity
  • Best fit for IREN: regional/customer-access edges, not a replacement for a dedicated dark-fiber-and-DWDM backbone

  • Signals: they may already suspect an underperforming vendor and want to see your evaluation process
"What redundancy/resiliency level does IREN target for different site types?"Discovery
  • Typical tiering: production sites N+1 minimum with two diverse carriers; pre-production can be single carrier; dev can be single circuit
  • Signals: they're testing whether you'll right-size investment instead of gold-plating everything
"How do customer SLA commitments drive the connectivity timeline?"Discovery
  • Customer SLAs should drive redundancy investment directly — get their commitments and work backward from go-live with buffer built in
  • Signals: they want to know if you connect technical decisions to business/customer commitments
"What's the annual connectivity budget, and how much flexibility exists for redundancy vs. cost optimization?"Discovery
  • Budget reality shapes the approach — smart diversification and phased redundancy if constrained, more aggressive ownership if there's room
  • Signals: they're gauging whether you can operate under real financial constraints, not just an ideal-world plan
"Will this role have direct reports, or is it an individual-contributor role?"Discovery
  • Either way, position yourself as the bottleneck-remover for connectivity — solo with strong carrier relationships, or building a team if scope allows
  • Signals: they're clarifying scope and want to see you're flexible either way
"How often are you dealing with multi-month delays, and what's the current tolerance?"Discovery
  • Monthly delays would be crisis-level, quarterly would suggest a systemic issue — either way, target getting delays under 10% of projects
  • Signals: they're revealing how bad the current problem actually is
"What metrics define success in this role at 6, 12, and 24 months?"Discovery
  • 6 months: full visibility, SLA monitoring live, backup carriers activated, zero emergency escalations
  • 12 months: measurable availability improvement, faster deployment cycles, optimized cost
  • 24 months: infrastructure ready for the next order-of-magnitude of growth, connectivity no longer the blocker
  • Signals: they want to see you think in outcomes, not just activities
"What's the most important thing I need to know about succeeding here that isn't in the job description?"Discovery
  • Honest answer: connectivity is currently blocking growth, and this role succeeds by relentlessly forcing carriers to move faster than they naturally would
  • Signals: this is their most candid question — listen closely to what they say back
"Who would this role work closest with — Network Engineering, Infrastructure, Procurement?"Discovery
  • Closest with Infrastructure (deployment timelines), technical guidance from Network Engineering, contracts through Procurement — you're the hub translating between all three
  • Signals: they're testing whether you understand you're a coordinator, not a solo operator
"How does the renewable-energy mission affect carrier selection and infrastructure placement?"Discovery
  • Factor in whether carriers can commit to renewable-powered infrastructure and are present in renewable-rich regions — a potential strategic differentiator
  • Signals: they're checking whether you did real research on the company, not just the role
"What's the carrier landscape in our primary regions?"Discovery
  • General framework: Tier 1 carriers (reliable, expensive, everywhere) vs. regional carriers (competitive, strong locally) vs. dark fiber specialists (best for long-haul)
  • Signals: they want to see a structured carrier-evaluation framework, not just name-dropped vendors
